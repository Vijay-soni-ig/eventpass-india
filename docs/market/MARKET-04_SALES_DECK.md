# Slide 12 — Discovery Before Demo

### Ask first

#### Event profile

- What types of exhibitions/events do you run?
- How many events do you typically run per year?
- What is the typical event size?
- How many exhibitors do you manage?

#### Operations

- How do you currently manage stalls?
- How do you collect exhibitor payments?
- How do visitors register and buy tickets?
- How is check-in handled?
- How are exhibitor leads captured?

#### Business

- Which workflow consumes the most manual effort?
- Where do errors or duplicate records occur?
- Which numbers do you need after an event?
- What would make a new platform worth adopting?

#### Timing

- What is your next event?
- When does exhibitor sales/booking begin?
- When does visitor registration open?

# Slide 13 — Qualification Framework

A prospect is commercially relevant when several of these are true:

- Runs recurring exhibitions/events
- Manages multiple exhibitors
- Has stall allocation or exhibitor participation
- Sells visitor tickets or registrations
- Needs digital check-in
- Needs exhibitor lead capture
- Has measurable event reporting needs
- Has an upcoming event suitable for beta evaluation

### Do not qualify solely on event size.

Operational complexity can be more important than headline attendance.

# Slide 14 — Common Objections

### "We already use spreadsheets."

Response:

> That is common. The useful question is which parts of the workflow should remain manual and which should become connected. Let's map your current event process first.

### "We already have a ticketing platform."

Response:

> ExhibitTix is broader than visitor ticketing. The organizer workflow also covers exhibitors, stalls, event operations, check-in, leads, and analytics. We can identify where your current stack ends and where ExhibitTix could fit.

### "Our team is comfortable with our current process."

Response:

> Then we should not replace something that works without evidence. Let's identify one operational workflow where the current process creates friction and evaluate that workflow first.

### "Can you guarantee ROI?"

Response:

> We should measure that with your real event data rather than promise a percentage before the baseline exists.

### "Is this production ready?"

Response:

> Current capabilities should be presented according to their verified release/beta status. Do not represent roadmap functionality as shipped functionality.

# 17. Sales Talk Track

## 30-second pitch

> ExhibitTix is an exhibition and event management platform built for organizers. It connects the operational lifecycle — from event setup, venues, exhibitors and stall bookings to visitor ticketing, QR check-in, lead capture and analytics. Instead of treating each workflow separately, the platform keeps the event data connected in one operational system.

## 2-minute pitch

> Most event operations involve several connected workflows: setting up the event, managing exhibitors, allocating stalls, collecting payments, registering visitors, issuing tickets, checking people in, capturing exhibitor leads and reporting results afterward.
>
> ExhibitTix is designed around that lifecycle. Organizers can structure the event, manage exhibitors and stalls, run ticketing and QR check-in, capture leads and review operational and financial performance.
>
> The goal is not to claim that every external tool disappears immediately. The goal is to give the organizer a connected operational platform and identify the workflows where it creates measurable value.
>
> We are currently building a beta group with organizers so the product can be validated against real event operations.

# 18. Sales Rules

### Always

- Lead with the organizer's problem.
- Ask discovery questions before demonstrating features.
- Demonstrate connected workflows.
- Use verified product capabilities.
- Be explicit about beta/production status.
- Discuss pricing transparently.
- Record objections and workflow gaps.
- Capture the prospect's next event and timeline.

### Never

- Invent customer logos.
- Invent usage numbers.
- Claim unsupported ROI.
- Claim "India's #1" or similar market leadership.
- Present roadmap features as shipped.
- Promise integrations that are not verified.
- Promise custom features without product/engineering confirmation.
- Treat a demo request as a qualified opportunity without discovery.

---

# 19. Proof Strategy

Sales proof should mature in this order:

1. Verified product screenshots
2. Verified workflows
3. Organizer discovery interviews
4. Beta organizers
5. Real event case studies
6. Measured outcomes
7. Testimonials with permission

Every external claim should be classified as:

- **Verified**
- **Beta**
- **Roadmap**
- **Marketing opinion**

Only current verified/beta-qualified capabilities should be represented as available product functionality.
